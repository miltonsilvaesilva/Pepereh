## What happened
From 9 to 12 November 2026, nine Angolan startups took part in Web Summit in Lisbon, one of Europe's largest technology and entrepreneurship conferences. Their participation was supported by Angola's National Institute for the Support of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (INAPEM), in collaboration with the International Finance Corporation (IFC), part of the World Bank Group, with funding from the European Union. The initiative is part of the IFC-led Incubators project.

The stated goal is internationalisation: giving Angolan companies direct access to investors, potential partners and new markets. It is one of several consecutive editions in which Angola has brought a delegation of startups to the event.

## Why it matters
Access to finance remains one of the main obstacles to the growth of Angolan startups, especially in their early stages. International events of this scale do not guarantee investment, but they create something the domestic market still offers little of: contact with specialised investors, comparison with solutions from other countries, and visibility among potential customers and partners.

Angola's regular presence also helps build a reputation. When an ecosystem shows up consistently, it stops being a curiosity and starts being followed by funds, accelerators and companies looking for local partners.

## What it means for companies and institutions in Angola
For established companies, startups are increasingly a practical source of innovation: solutions in payments, logistics, agriculture, education or digital services can be tested and integrated with less investment than in-house development. It is worth following who is selected for these programmes and creating channels for collaboration, such as pilot projects or supply partnerships.

For entrepreneurs, the lesson is preparation. Anyone meeting investors needs a clear business model, reliable indicators, organised documentation and a value proposition that can be understood in a few minutes. That structuring work starts long before the event.

For public institutions and development partners, the continuity of these programmes — linking incubation, training and market access — is what turns one-off participation into lasting results.
