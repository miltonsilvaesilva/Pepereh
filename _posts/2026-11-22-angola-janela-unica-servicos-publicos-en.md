## What happened
Angola is building the technology platform for its **Single Window for Remote Public Services** (Janela Única de Prestação Não Presencial de Serviços Públicos), designed to let citizens and companies handle public administration processes remotely, without travel, queues or paper.

According to the Angolan press, the President authorised spending of about 4.31 million dollars to acquire and implement the platform, through an order published in the official gazette on 27 August 2026, which also authorises the corresponding public tender. The order calls for high standards of security and digital sovereignty, a single unified digital channel to public administration services and the ability for citizens to track their requests.

The measure is part of the Angola Digital Acceleration Project (PADA), financed under an agreement between the Ministry of Finance and the World Bank. The Single Window was established by Presidential Decree No. 196/22 of 22 July, which assigns coordination to the Administrative Modernisation Institute (IMA).

## Why it matters
Today, many processes require visiting several counters and handing the same documents to different bodies. A Single Window changes that logic: the request enters through one point and the systems of the various bodies exchange the information among themselves.

For this to work, **interoperability** is essential — the ability of different systems to communicate securely and in an organised way. The press has also reported the authorisation of a public administration interoperability platform under the same project. Separately, the government has said the Single Logistics Window, which will track freight by rail, road, sea and air, is in its final phase.

## What it means for companies and institutions in Angola
For companies, a single digital channel can cut the time and cost of processes such as licensing, registrations and document requests, and make dealings with the state more predictable. It is worth preparing now: organising documentation in digital form and reviewing internal processes that depend on responses from public administration.

For public institutions, the challenge is integration: connecting existing systems, ensuring data quality, protecting citizens' information and supporting the change with staff training. Success is measured by how simple the service is for the people who use it.
