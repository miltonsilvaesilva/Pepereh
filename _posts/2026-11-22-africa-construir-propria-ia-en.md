## What happened
On 17 November 2026, the 29th Africa Tech Festival opened in Cape Town, one of the continent's largest technology gatherings, running until 19 November. In a virtual opening address, South Africa's Deputy President Paul Mashatile argued that Africa must move beyond consuming technologies developed elsewhere and build its own digital capabilities to compete in the global artificial intelligence economy.

Mashatile called for AI frameworks that protect rights and reflect African languages and development priorities, and stressed that the continent's most valuable resource in the digital era is its human talent.

## Why it matters
Artificial intelligence is changing how services are delivered, how decisions are made and how companies and governments are organised. When solutions are designed only outside the continent, they do not always take into account African languages, data, rules and market realities.

The debate on digital sovereignty — each country's and region's ability to control data, infrastructure and technology skills — is therefore gaining weight on public and business agendas across Africa.

## What it means for companies and institutions in Angola
For Angolan organisations, the message is practical: adopting AI matters, but understanding and controlling what is adopted is decisive. That means organising and protecting their own data, choosing solutions that integrate with existing systems and complying with the country's personal data protection framework.

Investing in talent is equally central. In-house teams able to assess, adapt and maintain technology solutions reduce dependence on third parties and increase the value of every investment.

For public institutions, regional cooperation on digital standards and skills can speed up the modernisation of citizen services.
