## What happened
On 9 November 2026, the Plenipotentiary Conference of the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) opened in Doha, Qatar, running until 27 November. It is the highest decision-making body of the United Nations agency for information and communication technologies: it meets every four years, sets the organisation's strategy and elects its leadership.

The Angolan delegation is led by the Minister of Telecommunications, Information Technologies and Social Communication, Mário Oliveira, who called for greater representation of African countries in global digital governance. Angola's stated priorities include innovation, cybersecurity, artificial intelligence and strengthening the institutional and technical capacity of African countries in the telecommunications sector.

## Why it matters
Decisions taken at the ITU shape radio-spectrum management, technical standards, cybersecurity cooperation and universal connectivity priorities. In practice, they affect the cost and quality of communications, the speed at which new technologies are adopted and how countries protect their digital infrastructure.

For Africa, where the digital access gap is still significant, having weight in these discussions is a condition for global rules to reflect the continent's needs.

## What it means for companies and institutions in Angola
Angola's position confirms that digital transformation is a state priority, with investment in connectivity, submarine cables and the space industry. For companies, this means an environment in which digitalisation is no longer optional: customers, regulators and partners will expect secure, interoperable digital services.

The emphasis on cybersecurity and artificial intelligence deserves special attention. Organisations that start now to assess their systems, protect data and set internal rules for the use of AI will be better prepared for new regulatory requirements and to compete in regional markets.

For public institutions, alignment with international standards makes cooperation, financing and cross-border service integration easier.
