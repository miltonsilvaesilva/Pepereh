## What happened
On 9 November 2026, South African fintech Yoco announced a 100 million dollar credit facility from private credit manager Victory Park Capital. The funds go to Yoco Capital, the company's arm that provides cash advances to merchants.

This is debt financing rather than an equity investment, so it does not set a new valuation for the company. According to Yoco, more than 6.5 billion rand has been advanced to over 40,000 merchants since 2018. Interest rates and the drawdown schedule were not disclosed.

## Why it matters
Yoco started by supplying card terminals and payment solutions to small businesses. Over time, the data from those transactions came to show how much and how regularly each merchant sells — information that makes it possible to assess risk and grant credit faster than traditional processes.

It is a trend visible across several African markets: payment platforms are becoming full financial platforms, and access to credit for small and medium-sized enterprises, historically difficult, increasingly depends on a digital track record.

## What it means for companies and institutions in Angola
In Angola, electronic payments have been growing, with solutions such as Multicaixa Express and the BNA's strategy for the payment system. The Yoco case shows the next step: when sales are recorded digitally, a business builds a history that can make it easier to access financing.

For SMEs, the message is practical: accepting digital payments and keeping invoicing organised is not just a convenience for customers — it also builds financial credibility. For banks and fintechs, there is room for credit products based on transaction data, always within the BNA's regulatory framework and data-protection rules.

For public institutions, models like this can support financial inclusion and the formalisation of the economy.
